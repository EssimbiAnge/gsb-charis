## From a child's growth to a community's future, one thing runs through it all: collaboration. That is what the visit of **Children's Mission Africa (CMA)** brought home to the <CharisName /> community earlier this month. For three years, the international organisation has supported, through the **MEPCAM** association, projects in the **Monou II (Monokoubo)** community in Bertoua. It has also supported parents and children who were struggling to cover school fees. In total, **[X]** parents and **[Y]** children have benefited so far. This time, CMA's team made the journey to see the fruits of that commitment first-hand, and to meet and encourage those who have benefited.

> ### **About Children's Mission Africa**

> CMA envisions a continent where everyone can live a life in dignity. Together with its partners, it works in nine African countries across thirteen sustainable development projects, strengthening local organisations. It is funded by Skandinaviska Barnmissionen (Children's Mission Sweden) and partners, all sharing one goal: improving the welfare of vulnerable African children and families.

## A visit that began with respect

<ArticleImageFromGallery locale="en" event="2026/cma-visite-a-cheferrie-de-monou-ii-oct-6" file="IMG_E6822.webp" />

Before meeting the families, the sixteen-member delegation, made up of CMA representatives from Kenya, <CharisName /> staff, and other partner organisations from various regions of Cameroon, first paid a visit to the chief of the Monou II (Monokoubo) community, at his compound on Tuesday 6 October at around 7:50 am. The aim was to greet the chief, ask his permission to move around the community out of respect for tradition, and acknowledge his contribution to supporting the work carried out on his land.

This community holds a special place in the partnership. For the past three years, Monou II has been at the heart of CMA-supported projects, including entrepreneurship trainings that bring together about 50 people each year, and a poultry project that equips about 20 people each year with training and a starter kit of chicks to launch their own businesses. A child is more likely to stay in school when their family can stand on its own feet. What CMA supports in homes and what <CharisName /> builds in classrooms are two sides of the same commitment.

<ArticleImageFromGallery locale="en" event="2026/cma-visite-a-cheferrie-de-monou-ii-oct-6" file="IMG_E6854.webp" />

Taking the floor, **Mr. Tom Owuor**, CMA's Africa Mission Leader, greeted the audience with "Jambo", the Swahili word for "hello", and brought greetings from Kenya, noting that the journey to Bertoua had taken nearly 24 hours. "Africa is big," he said, "rich in resources and good people." He reminded everyone that Africa is a young continent, with most of its people under 35, and that the child is its most exposed and vulnerable member. That is why this generation holds the foundation of the future. He described a global partnership linking Sweden, Kenya, Cameroon, and other African partners around a single goal: the future of the child.

<ArticleImageFromGallery locale="en" event="2026/cma-visite-a-cheferrie-de-monou-ii-oct-6" file="IMG_E6872.webp" />

**Mrs. Magdalene Kelel**, Program Coordinator for the Livelihoods Program and a member of Kenya's Maasai community, also spoke, to warmly thank everyone for the welcome and to share her joy at discovering a Cameroon she called "very beautiful and rich in resources."

<ArticleImageFromGallery locale="en" event="2026/cma-visite-a-cheferrie-de-monou-ii-oct-6" file="IMG_E6885.webp" />

The meeting ended with a very Kenyan gesture: presenting the chief with a pack of **Kericho Gold**, a tea from the Kericho region of Kenya, which Mr. Owuor considers the best in the world.

## A partnership that transforms

Brief as it was, this visit reflects the <CharisName /> mission: *transforming communities through quality education*. When a family is supported, a child can stay in school, and a child who learns grows into an adult capable of creating pertinent solutions for their community.

## Our partners

This collaboration rests on a network of partners, whom <CharisName /> sincerely thanks:

<ArticleImageFromGallery locale="en" event="2026/random_images" file="partners.jpeg" />

- **[The Navigators of Cameroon](https://navigatorscameroon.org/)**: a Christian discipleship ministry based in Yaoundé, and the umbrella organisation from which MEPCAM and PEC come.
- **[MEPCAM (Mission Enterprise Partnership Cameroon)](https://mepcam.org/author/mepcameroon)**: a branch of The Navigators of Cameroon, founded in 2008, which identifies, trains and establishes entrepreneurs through enterprise development training, mentoring and financial empowerment. CMA's partner on the ground, it now reaches from the South West to the East and Far North.
- **PEC (Parlons Entrepreneuriat Chrétien)**
- **Children's Mission Africa (CMA)**: working in nine African countries across thirteen sustainable development projects, with funding from Children's Mission Sweden and partners.
- **[B2THEWORLD (B2W)](https://www.b2theworld.org/)**: a US-based non-profit supporting education for children and communities affected by war, under the banner "Transformation Through Education."
- **[Global Partners for Education (GP4E)](https://gp4e.org/)**: provides educational resources to under-resourced communities, with a focus on teacher development and early childhood education. They join us later this month.

## What this partnership means for CHARIS

<ArticleImageFromGallery locale="en" event="2026/cma-visite-a-charis-oct-8" file="IMG_7294.webp" />

For a school, help with school fees is not only a financial matter. It is the continuity of a journey: a child who doesn't leave school mid-year is a child who builds on what they've learned, keeps their place in the classroom, and keeps faith in their future. It is also a message to families: you are not alone. By pairing support for children with training for parents, the partnership works at the root, where the decision to keep a child in school is often made.

This collaboration among Sweden, Kenya, Cameroon, and other African partners is also a living lesson for our pupils: leaders from different countries working together to solve a real problem. It is exactly the spirit of the <CharisName /> vision: *raising global leaders capable of creating pertinent solutions.*
