## <CharisName /> Bilingual School Complex welcomed parents and guardians to campus on September 24th for the first Parent-Teacher Association meeting of the new school year, presided over by Director Mrs. Tatiana and the school's Founder. The turnout spoke for itself — close to 140 parents and guardians filled the room, standing alongside all 24 members of staff, a clear sign of just how invested the <CharisName /> community is in the year ahead. The bilingual session — delivered in French and English, with English interpretation provided by Mrs. Bernadette — ensured every parent could follow along comfortably, regardless of their preferred language

## This Year's Theme: "Read, Write, Count"

The meeting opened by grounding the year ahead in a single shared theme: *"Read, Write, Count: A Shared Commitment, A Promise to Every Child."* The school explained that this theme grew directly out of what parents themselves have voiced — a desire to see their children grow not only academically, but in character: becoming responsible, honest, and capable of thinking and acting independently; a wish for children to be genuinely prepared for the world ahead; and a desire to feel more involved, informed, and equipped as partners in that journey, rather than on the sidelines of it.

## A Shared Vision for the Year

Academically, the goal is clear: by the end of the year, every child should be able to read, write, and calculate confidently, with monthly diagnostic evaluations tracking each pupil's individual progress along the way. In fact, an initial diagnostic evaluation in language and mathematics was already carried out during the very first week of school, giving teachers an early, individualized picture of where each child stands and what support they may need.

But success at <CharisName /> was framed just as much in terms of character — integrity, critical thinking, and collaboration — as it was in terms of grades, reflecting the school's belief that raising capable, grounded young people matters as much as academic achievement.

## A Partnership Built on Five Commitments

Central to the discussion was the idea that a child's success depends on a genuine partnership between school and home. Parents were invited to commit to five simple habits:

1. **Encourage reading** — setting aside regular, consistent time for it;
2. **Support writing** — encouraging their child to write and take care with their work;
3. **Make math part of everyday life** — using everyday situations to count, compare, and reason;
4. **Monitor schoolwork** — keeping track of notebooks, homework, activity books, and remedial work;
5. **Communicate** — promptly sharing any difficulties or useful information with the school.

## Parents Choose the First Training Topic

<CharisName /> also introduced a new monthly training program open to interested parents, held free of charge on a Saturday once a month. The program offers two tracks to choose from: an education and parenting track — covering things like understanding your child, discipline and boundaries, and communicating effectively as a family — and an entrepreneurship and financial independence track, covering financial literacy, the psychology of money, and budgeting.

Parents were invited to vote on which track to begin with — and the results are in: the majority chose to start with **Education and Parenting**, signaling strong interest in practical tools to support their children's growth at home. The first session is scheduled for October 17th.

## How the Year Will Unfold

The school also shared a look at how the first two months will build on each other. September is focused on laying the foundations — getting to know each child, assessing where they stand, establishing classroom routines, and identifying who may need extra support. October shifts toward consolidating that groundwork — weekly checks, remedial classes in reading and math beginning October 8th, extracurricular clubs launching October 14th, and the start of more targeted school-family follow-up, including home visits where needed.

Looking at the first few weeks overall, the school flagged a few areas it will be paying particular attention to across the school: reading and comprehension, writing and self-expression, numeracy, attention and engagement in learning, punctuality and attendance, care for learning materials and routines, and — as ever — school-family communication.

## Key Dates for October and Beyond

A full first-term calendar was shared with parents. School will be closed on October 5th for Teachers' Day, and the national exam registration window opens October 6th and runs through the end of November. Remedial classes begin October 8th, and October 14th marks both the deadline for the first tuition installment and the launch of extracurricular clubs. The first parent training session follows on October 17th, with the first monthly evaluation set for October 28–29th.

Looking further ahead: preparations for the Christmas tree celebration begin November 16th, first-term exams run November 24–26th, the second parent training session is November 28th, and national exam file submissions close November 30th. December brings end-of-term class councils (December 11th), the Christmas tree celebration alongside the start of the Nursery break (December 16th), primary report card distribution (December 18th), and the third and final parent training session before the holidays (December 19th).

## Administrative Reminders

Parents were also reminded of a few practical items: **the first tuition installment is due by October 14th**, and administrative files should be kept up to date — including medical certificates for Nursery admission, birth certificate copies, photos, and the required ream of paper. On uniform and appearance, the school asked parents to ensure shirts are tucked in, zippers are in good condition, and shoes are closed-toe, and reminded families to keep up with children's deworming. The school also noted that several children have reported stomach discomfort since returning from the break, and asked parents to stay attentive to this at home. Class timetables and the first-term calendar will be shared through class platforms, and reading remediation support remains available for children who need extra help, at 4,000 FCFA per child, payable in advance. Communication going forward will run through the general "Community Families" group and each class's dedicated group.
