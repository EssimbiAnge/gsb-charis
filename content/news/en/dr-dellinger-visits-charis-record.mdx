### This week, **Dr. LaNette Dellinger, PhD**, director of **Global Partners for Education (GP4E)**, travelled to the East Region of Cameroon all the way from the Republic of Georgia, to spend four days at <CharisName />. GP4E is one of the partner organisations that stand with the school, and from **Monday 26 to Thursday 29 October** she did something unusual for a visiting director: she sat in our classrooms, watched, listened, and taught.

> ### **About Global Partners for Education (GP4E)**

> GP4E provides high-quality educational resources to under-resourced communities, working toward a world where quality education is the norm, especially in marginalized communities. It partners with education ministries, universities, and public and private schools to support community programs and volunteer education initiatives, with a special passion for teacher development and early childhood education. Dr. Dellinger is also connected to The Navigators ministry.

Dr. Dellinger arrived at around 7:20 am on Monday 26 October, and the welcome began straight away. A pupil of **Class 3, [name]**, greeted her with a speech in English, thanking her for coming all the way from the Republic of Georgia to the East of Cameroon for the very first time, and adding that, for the day, "our classrooms are brighter, our songs are louder, and our smiles are wider."

She then joined the morning assembly, where she introduced herself to the pupils with a bit of theatre. Her name, she explained, comes with a "Dr.", but she is not a medical doctor. She is a professor who works with schools around the world where other boys and girls like them are learning, and her job, she said, is simple: *to help teachers, to help schools, and to help children.* With the school director translating into French, the pupils answered with a warm "Merci!" and "Welcome to <CharisName />!"

After the assembly, she was introduced to the staff, then set off on a tour of the campus with the school's founder, **M. Ange**, and the director, **Mme Tatiana**.

In the joint Class 5/6, Dr. Dellinger wanted to know how a single teacher manages two grades in one room, a question many schools around the world are asking. The nursery section won her heart: she took many photos of the children and could not hide how adorable she found them. She also watched the Nursery 1 pupils go through their morning routine of rhymes, songs and dances, from the window, since the tour did not yet take her into the classrooms.

In the music room, which doubles as the library, she was drawn to the traditional instruments, especially the xylophones, and the way each bar produces a different note.
    
In the ICT lab, the conversation turned practical, as Dr. Dellinger spoke about how GP4E might better support the school's ICT education efforts.

At around 8:30 am, she settled into Class 1 for an observation session, and that set the pattern for her stay. Over the following days she moved from class to class, sitting in on lessons to see what a day at <CharisName /> looks like, and chatting with each teacher afterwards. She also talked with teachers informally, between lessons.

What she saw earned her appreciation. She told the staff how positive, friendly and warm they are with their pupils, and commended the way they build children's sense of their own worth. She had watched one nursery class cheerfully declare "I am the best" and "I am intelligent", and noted that in the end, it matters more for a child to know they have value and dignity than to be able to count to a thousand.

On Wednesday, the visitor became the teacher. In the joint Class 5/6, Dr. Dellinger taught a lesson on **animal conservation**, using gorillas as her subject. She began with printed pictures of gorilla species, which pupils stuck on the board themselves, and had them guess the topic before revealing it. She then taught key words such as "impressed", "disgusting" and "humming" by acting them out, and gave each grade its own passage: an article on the endangered gorillas of the Congo Basin* “Safe at Home?”,*for Class 5, and a short zoo story, "Say Hello to the Giant Gorilla", for Class 6. Class 5's exercise (share a cool fact about gorillas, and explain why they are endangered) gave Class 6 the background for their story, so the two grades learned from each other. Pupils took part throughout.

On the final day, Dr. Dellinger took a group photo with the staff and administration, then sat down with the teachers for a **round table on teaching strategies and materials**. She brought more than ideas: she presented a collection of materials for the school, and went through ways to use each one.

She started by making clear that everything she would show them was a suggestion to adapt, not a rule to follow. When trainings or materials arrive, she said, teachers must decide for themselves what works in their own classrooms. She also acknowledged the challenge of very large classes and limited resources, with genuine praise for what teachers are already doing.

**Made to last:** The first showpiece was a **laminator**, with some lamination film. She walked teachers through the steps: print in colour, slide the page into the plastic sheet, and feed it through the heated machine until it comes out as a durable card. Laminated materials are waterproof and wipe-clean, so a child's dirty finger or a sneeze is no longer a problem. Teachers can laminate their existing hand-drawn wall posters instead of redrawing them, and create new flashcards and games that will last. She also shared a **free website of printable teaching resources**, with thousands of sets covering colours, numbers, shapes, family members, days of the week, months and seasons for teachers to download and print.

**Books and reading:** She brought **picture storybooks** for the lower classes, **longer novels** for the upper classes, **read-along books** for teachers, and a **science experiments book** with easy activities using household items like baking soda and lemon juice. Her advice was to read aloud, even to older pupils, for just five or ten minutes after break, so that children learn that reading is fun and not only a drill. She modelled it with Eric Carle's *Rooster's Off to See the World*: teach a few key words first, ask pupils to predict what the book is about, and weave in counting along the way.

**Tools for hands-on learning:** The rest of the table was a treasure chest:

- **Air-dry modelling clay**, soft enough for the little ones, for making insects or animals
- **Pointer fingers** and **soft foam letters** for spelling names and simple words
- **Answer buzzers** for team review games, and **handheld whiteboards** for "show me" responses, and foam lesson dice you can write on
- **Number-matching cards**, **maths flashcards** and **dominoes**
- **Puzzles**, a **fabric body-parts model** and **soft farm animals** for early language learners
- **Thermometers, rulers and a measuring tape**, plus **number and alphabet charts**
- **Lesson dice and drawing boards** for pupils

She reminded the teachers that young children learn through their hands, and suggested rotating small groups through different activities every ten or twenty minutes. Teachers were invited to tell Mme Tatiana what else they need, so the school can look for it, including locally where possible.

As the last session drew to a close, school leadership spoke about how the visit came together. After a single video call, a promise to come, a visa and a flight, Dr. Dellinger was at <CharisName /> within roughly three months. For a school that had been praying for partners, it felt like an answer, and leadership acknowledged that she had given up time and money and stepped well outside her comfort zone to be with us.

True to our culture of hospitality, <CharisName /> presented her with gifts of its own: a **staff tracksuit**, a **plaque** with the school's name and logo, a **set of mugs** for her and the GP4E team, and a **framed photo** of her and the team. The session ended in prayer, and Dr. Dellinger left before sunrise on Friday morning for Yaoundé, on her way back to Georgia.

---

Great teaching is shared across borders, one classroom at a time. A visitor came to listen first, taught a lesson every teacher could borrow from, and left the school with tools to make the next one even better. This is the heart of what <CharisName /> means by **collaboration**, and of our vision of *raising global leaders capable of creating pertinent solutions*.
