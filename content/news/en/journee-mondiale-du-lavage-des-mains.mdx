## It is one of the simplest habits in the world, and one of the most powerful. Washing hands with soap helps prevent diarrhoea and respiratory infections, the very illnesses that keep children out of class and put families under strain. To mark **Global Handwashing Day (15 October)**, <CharisName /> dedicated the week to this habit, from classroom conversations to a hands-on exhibition and demonstration.

## A whole week of reminders

Throughout the week, teachers were encouraged to bring up handwashing in their classes: why germs spread, why soap matters, and why clean hands protect not only the child but also classmates and family. On Thursday 15 and Friday 16 October, the school then turned talk into practice with a handwashing demonstration.

## How to wash your hands properly

Most of us were taught to rinse quickly. Washing well takes a little longer, and these steps are what pupils practised:

1. **Wet** your hands with clean water.
2. **Soap** up, enough to make a lather.
3. **Scrub** for at least 20 seconds, covering the palms, the backs of the hands, between the fingers, the thumbs, and under the nails.
4. **Rinse** thoroughly under clean running water.
5. **Dry** with a clean cloth or let them air dry.

A handy trick for young pupils: hum a favourite song twice through while scrubbing, and you will have reached 20 seconds.

## When to wash: the key moments

- Before eating or handling food
- After using the toilet
- After playing outside or touching shared objects
- After coughing, sneezing, or blowing the nose
- When hands are visibly dirty

## How parents can help at home

A habit sticks when it is practised everywhere, not only at school. A few small steps make a big difference:

- Keep **soap and clean water** next to the toilet and the eating area.
- Make handwashing a **family routine** before meals, and do it together with your child.
- **Praise** the habit and be patient with young children, who need reminding.
- If soap and running water are not available, wash with clean water and soap in a basin or bucket, rinsing with fresh water, rather than skipping it.

## A habit that builds character

Handwashing may seem small, but it reflects values we care about at <CharisName />: discipline, responsibility for oneself and for others, and care for the community. A child who learns to wash their hands learns that small, consistent choices protect everyone around them.
