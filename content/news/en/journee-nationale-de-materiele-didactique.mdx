## This Friday, Groupe Scolaire Bilingue <CharisName /> joined schools across the country in celebrating National Didactic Material Day. Held each year in early October, in the lead-up to World Teachers’ Day, the observance celebrates the creativity of teachers and their ability to turn local and recycled materials into effective learning tools.

At <CharisName />, teachers, pupils, and even administrative staff rose to the challenge with enthusiasm. From the nursery section to the primary classes, each section presented materials made with their own hands, covering science, geography, numeracy, and ICT.

## Science in action

Among the standout presentations was one by Mrs. Nguepi and her CM1 pupils on electrical circuits. Using two wired boards fitted with bulbs and switches, and connected to a power outlet for the demonstration, the pupils showed first-hand the difference between a series circuit and a parallel circuit. It was a great example of learning through direct observation.

Pupils of Class 4, guided by Mrs. Kimbi, presented a miniature model of the digestive system. Cut-outs of the organs (mouth, lungs, intestines and more) were mounted on a board and linked by a thin tube, so a coloured liquid could trace the journey of food and fluids through the body, making the process easy for everyone to visualise.

In the same spirit, a pupil from Class 3 presented a model of the urinary system. The kidneys, ureters, bladder, and urethra were arranged on a board and connected by a network of piping, clearly illustrating how fluids travel through the body.

<ArticleImageFromGallery locale="en" event="2026/journee-nationale-de-materiele-didactique-oct-9" file="IMG_7553.webp" />

The school director, Mrs. Tatiana, presented a lung model herself: a sealed container with a base made from a torn balloon, and two balloons attached through a straw. Pulling the base inflates the “lungs,” and releasing it deflates them, simulating inhalation and exhalation. One of the two lungs was deliberately smaller, representing the lung of a smoker or a person with tuberculosis, a simple and striking way to teach pupils about the dangers of smoking.

## Measuring, comparing, counting

Mr. Ali, CE1 teacher, presented a handmade two-sided scale. Using a phone and a roll of toilet paper, he showed pupils how to compare the weight of different objects, turning an abstract idea into something instantly tangible.

The school secretary, Mrs. Kamba, also took part, with a number game designed to help nursery, SIL and Class 1 pupils learn numbers while playing.

The nursery section was fully involved too. Nursery 1 presented a numbers and colours sorting game made from polystyrene plates (disposable tableware), bottle caps in different colours, and wooden shapes. It was a simple, recycled, and colourful tool, ideal for building colour and number recognition in the youngest learners.

<ArticleImageFromGallery locale="en" event="2026/journee-nationale-de-materiele-didactique-oct-9" file="IMG_E7542.webp" />

Nursery 2 offered a numbers and shapes counting board along with a wooden ruler, introducing children to counting and their first notions of measurement.

## Discovering Cameroon

Geography was also well represented, with several maps of Cameroon: SIL pupils presented the cultural regions, CE2 the regions and mountains, and Class 3 the country’s diversity.

## ICT joins in too

The ICT lab also offered its own activity: a matching game where pupils paired each computer component with its function and its class, a playful way to discover how the machine works.

<ArticleImageFromGallery locale="en" event="2026/journee-nationale-de-materiele-didactique-oct-9" file="IMG_7556.webp" />

## Learning by doing

The day was a reminder of something <CharisName /> strongly believes: learning is richer when it is hands-on, visual, and participatory. With simple, often recycled materials, teachers and pupils showed that a good idea can matter more than expensive equipment. It also showed that creativity has no age limit: from nursery children to seasoned teachers, everyone contributed. That creativity in service of learning is at the heart of our mission: **transforming communities through quality education**.
