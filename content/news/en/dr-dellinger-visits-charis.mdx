*From 26 to 29 October, <CharisName /> welcomed Dr. LaNette Dellinger, Director of Global Partners for Education (GP4E), from Georgia to the East Region of Cameroon. Four days to discover the school, share in the everyday life of its classrooms and give shape to a shared ambition: working together to advance quality education for children.*

### It all began with the words of a pupil.

  src={'https://pub-e7da03db1ea14127b211181d60918407.r2.dev/2026/26-29-oct-dr-dellinger-visits-charis/IMG_E7938.webp'}

On Monday morning, one of our pupils welcomed the visitor in English and thanked her for travelling such a long distance to meet them. He then added a sentence that would set the tone for the entire visit: with her presence, “our classrooms are brighter, our songs are louder, and our smiles are wider.”

Dr. Dellinger's response was just as warm. Addressing the assembled pupils, she explained with a touch of humour that her title of “Dr.” did not mean she was a medical doctor. Her work, she explained, was about helping teachers, schools and children. Mrs. Tatiana, <CharisName />'s Director, translated her words into French, as the children responded with enthusiastic “Thank you!” and “Welcome to <CharisName />!”

The first connection had been made. The visit could begin.

### Stepping into the Life of the School

Accompanied by Mr. Ange, <CharisName />'s Founder, and Mrs. Tatiana, Dr. Dellinger began exploring the campus. Each space opened up a new conversation: How can teachers effectively teach two grade levels in one classroom? How can ICT education be strengthened? What role can music and traditional instruments play in learning?

The balafons (xylophones) caught her attention. In the nursery section, the children's songs, dances and enthusiasm also made a lasting impression.

But it was by sitting in the classrooms that she truly began to discover <CharisName />. From that first morning and throughout her stay, she observed lessons, listened and exchanged ideas with teachers, both during scheduled discussions and in the moments between lessons.

She particularly highlighted the warmth of the relationships between teachers and pupils, as well as the way educators nurture children's confidence. In one nursery class, she heard the little ones joyfully declare, “I am the best” and “I am intelligent.” She recognised in this an essential part of learning: an understanding of one's own worth and dignity, which matters just as much as academic knowledge.

### A Lesson on Gorillas, an Experience to Share

On Wednesday, Dr. Dellinger moved from observing lessons to leading one herself. In the combined Class 5 and Class 6 classroom, she delivered a lesson on gorillas and their conservation.

Pictures sparked the pupils' curiosity. They placed them on the board and tried to guess the lesson's subject. Gestures and facial expressions made English vocabulary easier to understand. Then each grade explored a different reading passage: one group learned about the threats facing gorillas in the Congo Basin, while the other read a story set in a zoo.

The activities complemented one another: what one group discovered helped the other understand. The pupils remained actively involved from beginning to end.

The session offered teachers a practical example of what can be achieved in a multi-grade classroom: activities adapted to each level, brought together around a common theme.

### Resources to Carry the Learning Forward

On the final day, teachers gathered around Dr. Dellinger for a session of pedagogical exchange and a closer look at the teaching materials she had brought.

Picture books, novels, counting games, foam letters, individual whiteboards, puzzles, measuring instruments and hands-on learning materials: each resource became a starting point for an idea that could be tried in the classroom.

A laminator would also make it possible to protect teaching aids and reuse them over time. A collection of science experiments opened up possibilities for activities using everyday objects. The books offered an invitation to establish a simple classroom routine: a few minutes of reading aloud, even with older pupils, to help them discover the pleasure of reading.

Throughout the discussions, Dr. Dellinger emphasised that teachers were free to adapt these ideas. The materials were resources to be explored and made their own, according to the needs of their pupils and the realities of their classrooms. She acknowledged the challenges of large class sizes and limited resources, while also recognising the work teachers were already doing.

This approach reflects GP4E's priorities: supporting access to quality education, particularly in underserved communities, with a focus on teacher development and early childhood education.

### A Relationship Taking Root

Just a few months earlier, there had been only an initial video call and a promise to visit. That promise had now become a journey, shared experiences, familiar faces and time spent together.

For <CharisName />, which had been praying for opportunities to connect with partners, Dr. Dellinger's presence carried a special significance. It reflected a personal commitment built on time, generosity and trust.

During the closing moments, the school presented its guest with several <CharisName />-branded keepsakes, including a framed photograph with the team. A prayer brought the gathering to a close before her departure for Yaoundé in the early hours of Friday morning.

These four days have given the long-term partnership that <CharisName /> and GP4E hope to build a first tangible shape. Needs are better understood, ideas have been exchanged and relationships have grown stronger.
